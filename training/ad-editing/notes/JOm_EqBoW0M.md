## One-line thesis
Professional video editing is driven not by thousands of random sound effects, but by mastering three core SFX archetypes—**Whooshes** (motion/transitions), **Risers** (tension/anticipation), and **Impacts** (punctuation/release)—augmented by **Clicks** (tactile rhythm) and layered together to dictate audience emotion.

## Editing techniques

### 1. Motion Accentuation via Directional Whooshes
- **What it is:** Synchronizing a whoosh sound effect with on-screen physical subject movement or rapid camera reframing.
- **When to use:** Subject sprinting across the frame, throwing an object, fast horizontal whip pan, rapid push-in/zoom, or upward tilt reveal.
- **Exact parameters:**
  - *Cut duration / timing:* Sound peak aligns precisely at the moment of highest velocity across the lens center ([01:20]–[01:36]).
  - *Audio sync:* Lead-in whoosh starts ~6–12 frames (0.2–0.4s) prior to the action entering/moving, cresting at frame 0 of the motion climax.
  - *Camera moves shown:* 
    - Rapid 180° pan left-to-right ([01:32]).
    - Quick snap zoom-in (~100% to ~135% scale over 4–6 frames) ([01:33]).
    - Upward tilt from floor to seated subject ([01:36]).

### 2. Seamless Cut Smoothing (J-Cut / Transition Whoosh)
- **What it is:** Placing a whoosh across a hard scene transition cut to blend two disparate locations into one continuous movement.
- **When to use:** Transitioning from an indoor desk shot to an outdoor street/urban shot ([01:44]).
- **Exact parameters:**
  - *Audio tail overlap:* Start whoosh 8–10 frames before the cut; let the tail decay 6–8 frames into the incoming clip.
  - *Visual effect:* Masks the harshness of a hard cut by giving the auditory illusion of a rapid whip/swish camera move.

### 3. Tension Build via Risers & The "Smash Cut to Silence"
- **What it is:** A continuous upward-pitching or swelling sound (sub-bass rumble, cymbal swell, or synthesized riser) that suddenly terminates at a cut into complete silence or an extreme contrast shot.
- **When to use:** Pre-reveal moments, big scene changes, or punchline/climax delivery.
- **Exact parameters:**
  - *Riser build duration:* 1.5s to 3.5s gradual swell ([03:27]–[03:45]).
  - *Smash cut:* Cut video on the exact frame the riser hits peak volume. Drop audio immediately to -∞ dB (dead silence) on frame 1 of the new shot ([03:41]–[03:44]).
  - *Contrast ratio:* From loud build-up in a dynamic shot (handshake/action) to an extreme wide static shot (lone figure sitting in desert silence) at [03:44].

### 4. Visual Punctuation via Impacts
- **What it is:** Deep sub-bass hits, trailer impacts, or sharp punch/kick transients timed to visual contacts or major scene shifts.
- **When to use:** Physical object contact (door slam at [05:05], bag hitting the ground at [05:07]), text reveal, or bold graphic cuts.
- **Exact parameters:**
  - *Deep Impact:* Sub frequencies (~30–80 Hz) with a 1.5–2.5s decaying reverb tail ([05:47]).
  - *Sharp Hit / Kick:* Fast transient (<0.1s attack, <0.4s decay) directly on the frame of impact ([05:54]).

### 5. Multi-Layer SFX Stacking (Rumble Riser + Cymbal Riser + Impact)
- **What it is:** Combining low-end tonal rumbles with high-end bright textures to build complex emotional momentum.
- **When to use:** Prior to high-stakes visual reveals or major brand statements.
- **Exact parameters:**
  - *Layer 1 (Bottom):* Low rumbling bass riser (felt around 40–100 Hz) to create underlying dread/weight ([06:32], [06:42]).
  - *Layer 2 (Top):* High-frequency cymbal riser/shimmer (~4 kHz–16 kHz) providing acute urgency ([06:34], [06:42]).
  - *Climax:* Hard impact on frame 0 of the new shot ([06:24], [06:44]).

### 6. Tactile UI Rapid Montages (Metric/Product Pop on Clicks)
- **What it is:** Cutting micro-clips, screen crops, or graphic metric calls at high speed locked 1:1 with mechanical click transients (mouse, camera shutter, pen).
- **When to use:** Proof sequences, feature walk-throughs, or product detail showcases.
- **Exact parameters:**
  - *Pacing:* Rapid cuts every 8–15 frames (~0.3s–0.5s per cut) ([09:05]–[09:07]).
  - *Sync:* Exact cut on the transient of the mechanical click SFX.

## Sound design
- **Audio Mix Hierarchy & Levels (Observed on DAW meters at [00:14], [09:47], [09:57]):**
  - *Spoken Voiceover / Dialogue:* Peaks between -6 dB and -3 dB true peak. Centered, compressed, dry.
  - *Impacts / Climax SFX:* Peaks up to -1.0 dB to 0.0 dB true peak (brief, high-impact transients).
  - *Risers & Whooshes:* Swell from -18 dB up to -4 dB right before the cut.
  - *Background Music / Ambience:* Sits between -18 dB and -24 dB while voice is speaking; ducks by -4 dB to -6 dB during dense voice passages.
- **Core SFX Categories & Their Technical Execution:**
  1. *Whooshes:*
     - Heavy Whoosh: Low-pitched, heavy air displacement (~100–300 Hz dominant) for massive bodies moving fast ([01:54]).
     - Light Whoosh: High-passed (~800 Hz+), swift airy swish for quick pans and light objects ([02:11]).
     - Whip Whoosh: Ultra-fast transient spike (<150ms) for snappy comedic cuts and whip pans ([02:26]).
     - Wind Whoosh: Broad spectrum pink/white noise filtered soft sweep for aerial/drone b-roll ([02:45]).
  2. *Risers:*
     - Rumbling Riser: Low-frequency sine/sub swell (30–90 Hz), creates visceral gut tension without cluttering midrange dialogue ([03:59]).
     - Cymbal Riser: Reverse cymbal wash, bright high frequencies (3 kHz–12 kHz), adds acute urgency ([04:11]).
     - Shockwave Riser: Hybrid tonal riser escalating to a crescendo climax ([04:28]).
  3. *Impacts:*
     - Deep Impact: Low sub-bass thud (40–60 Hz) followed by a 2-second sub-boom tail ([05:47]).
     - Hit Kick / Punch: Hard mid-range slap (150–500 Hz) with immediate decay for physical contacts ([05:54]).
  4. *Clicks:*
     - Camera shutter, mouse clicks, mechanical switches; high crisp transients (2 kHz–8 kHz) with near-zero decay (<50ms) ([08:52]).
- **Ducking & Cutoff Rule:** On smash cuts, music and risers drop dead to absolute digital zero instantly; no lingering reverb tail unless an impact specifically provides it ([03:41]–[03:44], [05:32]–[05:36]).

## Mistakes / warnings
- **Sound Effect Bloat ([00:33]–[00:49]):** Downloading massive sound packs with tens of thousands of SFX causes "analysis paralysis." Editors waste hours auditioning sounds instead of cutting.
- **Over-Saturating with Heavy Sounds ([02:35]–[02:44]):** Using heavy, low-frequency whooshes on light, fast movements makes videos feel sluggish, overly self-serious, or melodramatic. Match tone: use light/whip whooshes for fast, witty, or casual edits; reserve heavy whooshes for cinematic gravitas.
- **Sound Disconnected from Picture:** Dropping sound effects randomly as "decoration." Every whoosh must attach to movement, every riser must lead to an expectation, and every impact must hit a visual landmark.

## Reproduction recipe with ffmpeg/scripts
*(Extrapolation based on techniques demonstrated in video)*

### 1. Generating a Smash Cut with Hard Audio Ducking
To cut from a high-tension riser to dead silence on a wide shot:

```bash
# Trim Clip A (A-roll building tension) to exactly 3 seconds, normalize audio
ffmpeg -y -i clip_a.mp4 -t 3 -af "volume=1.0" -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p temp_a.mp4

# Mute Clip B (Desert wide silence) for 2 seconds to create absolute drop
ffmpeg -y -i clip_b.mp4 -t 2 -an -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p temp_b_muted.mp4

# Add 2 seconds of silent audio track to clip B
ffmpeg -y -f lavfi -i anullsrc=r=48000:cl=stereo -i temp_b_muted.mp4 -shortest -c:v copy -c:a aac temp_b.mp4

# Concatenate for clean smash cut to silence
ffmpeg -y -i temp_a.mp4 -i temp_b.mp4 -filter_complex "[0:v][0:a][1:v][1:a]concat=n=2:v=1:a=1[v][a]" -map "[v]" -map "[a]" smash_cut.mp4
```

### 2. Automated Layered Whoosh + Impact on Video Cut (Python/ffmpeg)
```python
import subprocess

def add_transition_sfx(video_in, whoosh_sfx, impact_sfx, cut_time, output_file):
    """
    Offsets whoosh so it peaks at cut_time, drops impact exactly on cut_time.
    whoosh lead-in = 0.3s (300ms) before cut.
    """
    whoosh_start = max(0, cut_time - 0.3)
    impact_start = cut_time

    filter_complex = (
        f"[1:a]adelay={int(whoosh_start*1000)}|{int(whoosh_start*1000)},volume=0.8[whoosh];"
        f"[2:a]adelay={int(impact_start*1000)}|{int(impact_start*1000)},volume=1.0[impact];"
        f"[0:a][whoosh][impact]amix=inputs=3:duration=first:dropout_transition=2[aout]"
    )

    cmd = [
        "ffmpeg", "-y",
        "-i", video_in,
        "-i", whoosh_sfx,
        "-i", impact_sfx,
        "-filter_complex", filter_complex,
        "-map", "0:v",
        "-map", "[aout]",
        "-c:v", "copy",
        "-c:a", "aac",
        output_file
    ]
    subprocess.run(cmd, check=True)

# Example usage:
# add_transition_sfx("founder_ad.mp4", "light_whoosh.wav", "deep_impact.wav", 2.15, "founder_ad_sfx.mp4")
```

## Top 7 takeaways for editing Aura Miami founder and UGC ads

1. **Build a 3-Layer Universal Sound Palette (Kill SFX Overload):**
   Restrict your streetwear editing bin to 4 folders: *Whooshes* (Heavy, Light, Whip), *Risers* (Low Rumble, Bright Cymbal), *Impacts* (Sub-bass Booms, Punchy Slaps), and *Clicks* (Camera Shutter, Snap). This eliminates decision fatigue and enforces brand sonic consistency.
2. **Execute the "Whoosh-Into-Impact" Cut on Garment Transitions:**
   When transitioning from founder-speaking to a model wearing an Aura Miami hoodie or track pants, use a **Whip Whoosh** on the final 8 frames of the founder clip, landing dead on an **Impact** at the exact frame the product footage appears. This makes the apparel drop feel physical and premium.
3. **Punctuate Direct-Response Proof Hooks with Mechanical Clicks:**
   When showing order notifications, Shopify dashboard revenue, embroidery close-ups, or limited-drop inventory counts, cut between 3 fast shots (8–12 frames each) and sync each image change with a sharp **Camera Shutter or Click SFX**.
4. **Use Sub-Bass Rumbling Risers Beneath Founder Pain Points:**
   When the founder explains the problem with generic streetwear (cheap blanks, thin fabric, bad fits), run a subtle **Low Rumbling Riser** (-16 dB) under the voiceover. It generates subconscious physical tension without competing with the dialogue frequencies.
5. **Master the Smash Cut to Silence for Drop Announcements:**
   Build energy with a dual-layered riser (Rumble + Cymbal) up to the exact millisecond the new collection name or price appears, then instantly **cut to complete audio silence** on an extreme clean shot of the garment. The stark dynamic contrast forces 100% viewer focus on the product.
6. **Pre-Build "Transition Compounds" to Accelerate High-Volume Ad Outputs:**
   Combine your best risers, whooshes, and hits into 5–10 master transition `.wav` files. When editing 20 UGC hook variations a day, dropping one pre-timed compound onto cut points saves hours compared to manual micro-alignments.
7. **Pair Light/Whip Whooshes with Fast UGC Swipe and Unboxing Motions:**
   Never use heavy theatrical trailer whooshes on informal mobile UGC unboxings. Pair fast parcel ripping, box flipping, and phone camera tilts with **Light Whooshes** and **Whip Whooshes** to keep the ad feeling agile, kinetic, and native to TikTok and Instagram Reels feeds.
