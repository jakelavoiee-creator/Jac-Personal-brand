## 2. Persuasion mechanics: the 7 principles, applied truthfully
| Principle | Mechanism | Evidence | Aura application (truthful version) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocity** | We feel obliged to return what we were given first | Mints with the bill: 1 → +3% tips, 2 → +14%, a personalized "for you nice people" extra → **+23%** [kv0sOX6Alrk 02:25–03:03] | An **unexpected, personal** extra in the box (an unreleased sticker or keychain, a handwritten note). Free mindset and styling content before any ask. *Personalized + unexpected* beats bigger |
| **Scarcity** | We want what's rare or about to be lost; loss framing beats gain framing | British Airways announced the Concorde's end and sales jumped *the next day* [kv0sOX6Alrk 03:19–03:52]. A "limit 3 per person" sign doubled sales [cDREln_LrME 03:15] | Numbered runs and retired colourways, said plainly: "300 made, this dye is retired after this drop." The strongest form is **competition for a scarce item** (others are buying it right now) [cDREln_LrME 25:06]. Live counters only if they're real |
| **Authority** | Under uncertainty we follow credible experts (expertise + trustworthiness) | A receptionist introducing agents' credentials: **+20% appointments, +15% contracts** at zero cost [kv0sOX6Alrk 05:16–06:09] | Let **third parties** carry the credentials (press, a stylist, the mill, the artist), never self-praise [kv0sOX6Alrk 04:56]. Only real certifications |
| **Commitment / consistency** | We act in line with what we've actively and publicly committed to | A small window postcard → **4× more** agreed to a big yard sign [kv0sOX6Alrk 06:28–07:14]. "Will you call?" + pause → **−64%** no-shows [cDREln_LrME 11:15] | A **micro-commitment ladder** before drops: vote on the colourway → reserve with your phone number → "your reservation is set for 7:55 PM". | **Liking** | We say yes to people we like: similarity + genuine compliments | MBA negotiations: 55% → **90%** deals when parties shared something personal first [kv0sOX6Alrk 08:21–08:58] | Content that reflects the customer's own story back to them. | **Consensus (social proof)** | Uncertain people copy similar others | "75% of guests in **this room** reuse towels" beat a generic appeal by **+33%** [kv0sOX6Alrk 10:24–11:05] | The most **specific, similar** proof we truly have (real reviews from real Miami buyers, real re-order rates). ## 3. Holiday psychology (Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas)
→ Execution calendar: `../clothing-brand-ads/PLAYBOOK-DRAFT.md` §12. Sources: `web-notes.md` (videos still queued).
What's actually happening in the shopper's head, and the truthful Aura response:
| Force | What drives it | Aura move |
|---|---|---|
| **Loss aversion** | Missing a deal hurts about twice as much as the equal gain pleases (Kahneman–Tversky, cited via Fortune) | Frame every BFCM message around what they'd lose ("the last time this set is priced like this until spring"), *and keep that promise*. If we break it with a December sale, next year's BF stops working |
| **Competitive arousal** | Buying a scarce item in front of rivals feels like *winning* (Nichols, NKU) | Real competition made visible: real sell-out timestamps ("Black colourway gone in 41 min"), real waitlist size |
| **Unfairness backlash** | Perceived unfairness (bots, line-cutters, supply vanishing) produces anger (Lennon, Indiana) | **VIP early access must be fair**: one per customer on numbered pieces, bot protection, an honest restock policy. Fairness *is* brand equity here |
| **Commitment** | People follow through on what they committed to in advance (Cialdini) | VIP list ("*Will you* join…?"), then reserving a size, then a reminder: the ladder from §2 |
| **Unity** | Insiders get it first, so belonging becomes the reward | Early access is a members' perk, not a discount code |
| **Gift giving (Christmas)** | Buying for someone else lowers price sensitivity; the buyer wants to *look good* and *not get it wrong* (my inference; research still to source) | Gift guides, gift sets, a premium unboxing, **easy exchanges for size** (removes the fear of guessing wrong), a clear delivery-cutoff promise |

## 4. Pricing and AOV psychology *(videos still queued: Premium branding, Rory Sutherland ×2, Adam Erhart. Early principles below)*
- **Anchors and tiers:** single / 2-pack ("Most popular") / 3-pack ladders (Track A §6). In BF, "Buy 2 save 25%, buy 3 save 35%" beats a flat sitewide discount because it raises AOV while giving a deal.
- **Hold the price line on the core.** Supreme's margins come from full-price sell-through (Retail Dive). Constant sales train customers to wait (BitBranding's client fell from $1.5M to $1M a year). Rule: **the core never goes on sale**. BFCM value comes from bundles, gifts and access, not core markdowns.
- **Price as signal** (Veblen effect, to be confirmed by the queued sources): in identity categories a higher price can *raise* desire. That's a lever for the fragrance and eyewear tiers, never a reason to inflate the "was" price.

## 5. Drop and hype model *(videos queued: Supreme ×3, Corteiz ×2, Virgil ×2. Working model from web-notes)*
1. **Fixed ritual:** the same day and time every drop (Supreme: Thursday 11am). Ritual builds habit and anticipation.
2. **Make less than the demand on purpose**, then let the sell-out be the ad. Track A gives the real numbers: first run ~50 pcs, pre-agreed price breaks at 100/150 for the restock decision.
3. **Leak in small steps**: teaser → reveal → live (Track A §6 drop ritual), seeded through creators.
4. **Collabs as rocket fuel:** partners sell many times their usual volume through a collab (Outside). Aura for Artists is a collab engine by design.
5. **The tension to manage:** scarcity and growth pull against each other (NPD analyst, Retail Dive). The resolution is a tiered architecture (§6): scarce top tier for heat, accessible core for volume.
6. **Fairness rules** (§3): drop heat dies fast if fans feel cheated by bots or "limited" items that come back.

## 6. Brand architecture and extension rules *(videos queued: Gentle Monster, fragrance ×2, Warby Parker, Luxottica, Kith, Fear of God)*
**What the science says:**
- **Mental + physical availability** drive growth more than loyalty does (Byron Sharp). Aura needs **distinctive assets** used the same way for years (a mark, a colour, a phrase, a sonic cue), and to be **easy to find and buy** everywhere its buyers already are.
- An extension succeeds on **parent quality × fit** (Aaker & Keller): skills that transfer, complementary use, and not looking "too easy to make". **Dilution is real when an extension contradicts what the brand stands for** (Loken & Roedder John).

**Proposed architecture (for Jac to decide):**
```
AURA (master brand: the identity, the distinctive assets)
├── AURA MIAMI ............ core apparel: accessible-premium, the volume engine, always full price
├── AURA [top tier] ....... numbered/limited drops: heat, PR, scarcity (keeps the core desirable)
├── AURA FOR ARTISTS ...... collab engine (see the decision below)
├── AURA FRAGRANCE ........ high fit (identity + Miami sensory world); consider licensing once there's scale
└── AURA EYEWEAR .......... high fit (completes "the look"); a Gentle Monster-style brand world, not a commodity
```
- **Fit test for any new line:** (1) Does it express the same identity? (2) Would an Aura customer find it *natural*, not opportunistic? (3) Can we make it to the same quality bar? (4) Does it make the master brand **more** desirable? Four yeses or it doesn't ship.
- **Fear of God lesson:** a diffusion line (Essentials at ~$90–110 vs mainline $450–1,000+) can massively widen reach, but only because the mainline keeps the halo. Aura needs its top tier before it needs a cheaper tier.
- **Fragrance economics:** big houses license fragrance to specialists (Coty, Interparfums) for royalties plus entry fees (Lacoste ~€90M/15 yrs; Burberry £130M from Coty). For Aura now, a small private-label or indie launch builds proof. Licensing is the play *after* the brand has scale (royalty % still to source).
- **Aura for Artists economics:** merch partners typically keep 30–50%+ of sales; one model is 70/30 if the artist funds inventory, 50/50 if the partner carries production. Label 360 deals pay artists ~20–25% on merch. **Always define the base (gross, net or adjusted gross) and who pays production.**

**The key decision for Jac: what is Aura for Artists?**
| | A. Service (B2B: we make the artist's merch) | B. Collab line (Aura × Artist, B2C) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Production margin + a revenue share (30–50% partner share is common) | Aura retail margin; artist gets a royalty or rev share |
| Brand effect | Invisible: the artist's brand on the product | **Builds Aura**: both names, both audiences (Unity ×2) |
| Risk | Ops-heavy, cash tied up in inventory, low brand equity | Brand risk if the artist doesn't fit |
| My lean | Phase 2, once production ops are proven | **Phase 1**: it's the Supreme/Kith collab engine and builds the master brand |

### 9.1 The big tension, resolved for a print-on-demand brand
The hype brands (Supreme, Corteiz) win by **making less than demand** [g5VTZnhX5WU 02:26, 1yDhxRLMx3s 16:30]. Marketing science wins by **reach, ease of buying and light buyers** [G53MkkQ5dBo, akac_W-5N-4 02:16]. **Aura is print-on-demand: it can't sell out**, so "only 50 left" would be a lie.

**Aura's answer: availability for the core, scarcity only through time.**

| Layer | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Core** (Nine To Five, Created To Create…) | Always available, always full price, everywhere the buyer is. Built for reach | Sharp: penetration and light buyers drive growth; easy to buy wins |
| **Capsules** (new designs) | **Time-boxed:** "Capsule 02 closes Sun 23:59 ET; this design is retired forever." Then it really is gone | The only honest scarcity on print-on-demand. Supreme's loss aversion without a fake stock count |
| **Numbered runs** | Only once a hero item moves to small bulk production (2027): "300 made", and the number is real | Real limited supply, real resale value [dzq0nHoKtJ8 08:02] |

### 9.2 Distinctive assets: pick them and repeat them for years
Sharp: be recognised, don't chase "brand love" [G53MkkQ5dBo]. Virgil: repeat one template [l5dWVKiKRSs 03:19, DNZ5mPGW93Q 05:03]. Matt Gray: take away everything that isn't essential [6V4y0AX69h4 14:49]. Aura's set (proposed, Jac to lock):
1. The **AURA wordmark**.
2. **Black, white and one gold accent.**
3. **The back-print formula:** a statement in condensed caps with a small line beneath. This is what people recognise from across a room.
4. **"Too creative for nine to five"** as the brand line.
5. **The cross necklace** on Jack and Ava.

Every ad, page and box uses the same set.

### 9.3 The takeoff item
Virgil: find the one product you're known for and perfect it [DNZ5mPGW93Q 00:01]. **Aura's is the Nine To Five tee and hoodie.** It's the flagship of ads, photos and the homepage, and all 4 shots of it get done first.

### 9.4 Price framing and anxiety (Sutherland, Erhart)
- **Relative framing:** show the full look ($140 → $120 bundle) next to a single tee, and lead collection pages with the $100 hoodie, so the core reads as the sensible choice [lhlS-Wds02M 15:14, hYZG8qnS2T4 08:35].
- **Solve the anxiety, not the spec:** the purchase-killers are fit uncertainty, returns and "will this look right on me", so answer those on the page (model size line, size exchanges) [lhlS-Wds02M 06:53].
- **Reverse benchmarking:** make the parts competitors ignore memorable. A handwritten note, a surprise in the box, a beautiful shipping email, painless size exchanges [lhlS-Wds02M 31:33]. This confirms §2 reciprocity.
- **Explore/exploit:** spend about 80% of effort on the proven core and about 20% on bold experiments (a strange accessory, an unexpected collab) [lhlS-Wds02M 23:04, g5VTZnhX5WU 09:36].

### 9.5 When people buy, so ads show up at those moments (Sharp)
These are the moments someone decides to buy a piece like this:
- **First big paycheck / first client**
- **Quitting the 9–5**
- **A Miami trip or a big night out**
- **Starting a new chapter**
- **A gift for someone ambitious**

Map ad concepts to them [akac_W-5N-4]. The messaging stays broad enough for the whole category, not insider-only [akac_W-5N-4 04:32].
