## Marketing science: How Brands Grow (Byron Sharp / Ehrenberg-Bass)
- Brands grow mainly by **mental availability** (being thought of in buying situations) and **physical availability** (being easy to find and buy), reaching *more buyers*, rather than by deepening loyalty among a few [commentary summaries of the book: [Alex Murrell](https://alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/byron-sharp-how-brands-grow), [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/how-brands-grow/summary/), [E. Sharley Pt 2](https://sharley.substack.com/p/summary-how-brands-grow-part-2-by)].
- **Double jeopardy:** small brands have fewer buyers *and* slightly less loyal ones. Loyalty follows size, not the other way round.
- **Distinctive brand assets** (logo, colour, sound, character, slogan) work as memory cues. Recognition beats differentiation. They must be used **consistently over years**.
- **Pareto is softer than "80/20":** nearly half of sales come from the bottom 80% of customers (light buyers matter).
- *Verify exact figures in the book or Ehrenberg-Bass materials before quoting.*

## Scarcity and the drop model (Supreme)
- Weekly drop (Thursday 11am), most items sell out in under a minute; small runs per item by design [commentary: [MBS Group](https://www.thembsgroup.co.uk/internal/the-drop-a-lesson-in-artificial-scarcity-and-cool/); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/supreme_us)].
- Strong full-price sell-through and margins are tied to scarcity. An NPD analyst notes that **scarcity and growth pull against each other** [trade: [Retail Dive, VF buys Supreme for $2.1B](https://www.retaildive.com/news/vf-snaps-up-supreme-for-21b-eyes-brick-and-mortar-expansion/588621/)].
- Resale up to ~10× retail; information leaks in small steps through influencers [commentary/student: [Brown Political Review](https://brownpoliticalreview.org/supremes-pioneering-model/)].
- Collabs: one partner's drop item sold more than 10× its usual *annual* volume [trade: [Outside Business Journal](https://www.outsideonline.com/business-journal/brands/why-you-should-do-collabs/?scope=anon)].

## Black Friday and holiday psychology
- **Loss aversion:** losses weigh about twice as much as equivalent gains, and time-limited deals frame not buying as a loss [commentary citing Kahneman–Tversky: [Fortune, Tsipursky](https://www.fortune.com/2022/11/22/retail-psychological-tricks-black-friday-sales-deals-behavior-personal-finance-shopping-gleb-tsipursky); [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/consumption-and-lifestyles/202411/why-we-shop-on-black-friday)].
- **Consumer competitive arousal:** scarcity plus visible rivals makes shoppers feel buying is "winning" (Bridget Nichols, NKU). Perceived *unfairness* (line-cutting, supply running out) drives bad behaviour (Sharon Lennon, Indiana, n=189) [trade: [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/the-psychology-of-black-friday/10509235)].
- Urgency cues → emotional arousal, faster decisions, lower price sensitivity [commentary, unverified study: [Markopolo](https://www.markopolo.ai/post/the-psychology-of-black-friday-understanding-consumer-behaviour-and-decision-making)].
- **Gap:** gift-giving and mental-accounting research still to source (Journal of Consumer Research).

## Brand extension science
- **Aaker & Keller (1990):** an extension is judged on the parent brand's **quality**, the **fit** between categories (transfer of skills, complementarity, substitutability), their interaction, and how *hard* it seems to make [research summary: [Wikipedia: Brand extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_extension); replication: [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11002-009-9086-1)].
- Replications are mixed, but meta-analysis (Bottomley & Holden 2001) supports the full model; the weights vary by brand and culture.
- **Dilution:** Aaker & Keller found little; **Loken & Roedder John (1993) found dilution when the extension is inconsistent with the brand's beliefs** [research, secondary-cited].
- Reminding consumers of the parent strengthens its influence, for good or bad depending on fit [research: [JAMS 2018](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S11747-018-0586-5)].

## Fragrance licensing
- Fashion houses license fragrance to specialists (Coty, Interparfums), who develop, make and distribute it; the brand earns royalties and **entry fees**. Asset-light for the licensee [analyst: [Featherfund on Interparfums](https://featherfund.substack.com/p/interparfums-inc)].
- Entry payments can be huge: Lacoste–Interparfums reportedly €90M for 15 years; Coty paid Burberry £130M for exclusivity [trade: [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.in/news/business/lacoste-buys-back-fragrance-license-and-signs-with-interparfums-sa/2022122137712), [Modaes](https://www.modaes.com/empresas/burberry-da-marcha-atras-y-vuelve-a-licenciar-su-perfume-esta-vez-con-coty)]. Karl Lagerfeld moved Coty → Interparfums on a 20-year deal [trade: [Cosmetics Business](https://www.cosmeticsbusiness.com/karl-lagerfeld-ends-fragrance-license-with-coty-and-signs-with-interparfums--81652)].

## Artist merch economics (Aura for Artists)
- Merch companies typically take **30–50%+** of sales, depending on who funds production [commentary: [Vocal](https://vocal.media/fyi/the-hidden-goldmine-how-much-do-artists-earn-from-merchandising)]. One platform example: 70/30 if the artist buys inventory up front, 50/50 if the merch company carries production [trade: [Hype Magazine on Mercha](https://hypemagazine.co.za/?p=87730)].
- In label 360 deals the artist royalty is often ~20–25% (e.g. 22% of net on tees, 25% on D2C, 60% of net licensing) [trade: [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/360-record-deals-a-look-inside-multiple-rights-agreements-part-2/)]. Split structures: % of gross, tiered %, net-profit split, venue commission off the top [[Aaron Hall](https://aaronhall.com/merchandising-rights-live-venue-performance-contracts/)].
- Venues take a cut of table sales [[No Depression](https://nodepression.org/artists-push-back-to-keep-their-merch-table-money/)]. **Always ask: % of gross, net or adjusted gross? Who pays production?**
