- **Channel**: Skaleit

## One-line thesis
Scale clothing brands post-Andromeda by fixing website CRO, using broad-only targeting, segregating ad sets by AOV range to prevent spend-bias, and scaling winners through Bid Cap campaigns. [00:46]

## Method (ordered, step by step)
1.  **Foundation**: Ensure the brand has either a strong design aesthetic/quality or a built-in community (ideally both) [02:56].
2.  **Website Optimization**: Fix the site map, structure the product page for high conversion, and implement cross-sells/post-purchase upsells to maximize AOV [03:25].
3.  **Discovery (Campaign 1)**: Run a Broad, Dynamic Catalog campaign using all products to identify "Best Sellers" based on the current season/period [05:50].
4.  **Testing (Campaign 2)**: Create an ABO (Ad Set Budget Optimization) campaign. Split ad sets by AOV (Average Order Value) ranges (e.g., $50, $100, $150) to ensure Meta doesn't favor low-CPA/low-AOV items [06:35].
5.  **Scaling (Campaign 3)**: Move winning creatives into a Bid Cap campaign. Set specific bid caps for each AOV range to maintain a target ROAS (e.g., 3x) [09:35].
6.  **Creative Iteration**: Use the Meta Ads Library "Impression Filter" to research competitor winners and adapt those concepts for your brand [19:12].

## Meta ads specifics
-   **Account Structure**: Post-Meta Andromeda setup [00:15].
-   **Targeting**: **No exclusions, 100% Broad.** No interest or lookalike targeting [05:13].
-   **Campaign 1 (Discovery)**: 1 Ad Set, Broad targeting, Dynamic Catalog, All Products [05:50].
-   **Campaign 2 (Testing/ABO)**: Split Ad Sets by AOV range. Use 20–60 creatives per ad set consisting of all best sellers [06:35].
-   **Campaign 3 (Scaling/Bid Cap)**: 1 Ad Set per AOV range. Contains 6–15 winning ads from testing [09:35].
-   **Bidding Strategy**: Bid Cap is preferred over Cost Cap for more control over ROAS [14:15].
-   **Scaling Rules**:
    -   Increase budget by 20–30% every 2–3 days if ROAS > Target [13:24].
    -   If ROAS is significantly higher than target (e.g., 6x ROAS with a 3x target), double the budget immediately [13:41].
-   **Bid Cap Math**: If the goal is 3x ROAS and AOV is $100, the Bid Cap (Target CPA) should be ~$33 [10:02].
-   **Retargeting**: Do not use separate retargeting campaigns. Allow Meta to retarget automatically within broad campaigns by not using exclusions [17:58].

## Creative specifics
-   **Creative Volume**: High volume is required. Testing sets should have 20–60 creatives [08:45]. Scaling sets should have 6–15 active winners [12:30].
-   **Creative Types/Angles [20:16 - 26:26]**:
    -   **Aesthetics**: High-quality lifestyle shots in specific settings (e.g., "Old Money" style in a palace).
    -   **Attractive Reactions**: Short clips of people (e.g., a girl) reacting to the fit of the clothing.
    -   **Emotional Connection**: Ad copy focusing on how the clothes make the wearer feel or who they will attract.
    -   **Aesthetics Call Out**: Video showing the garment from all angles (front, side, back) with simple text overlays like "Shop Now."
    -   **Audience Style Call Out + Outfit**: "How to dress [Style Name]" videos showing a full outfit to increase cross-sells.
    -   **Founder/Behind the Scenes**: Storytelling videos about the brand's mission or quality.
    -   **Clothing on Ground (Flat Lays)**: Simple photos of clothes laid on the floor. Surprisingly high performance [40:01].
    -   **UGC Photos**: Simple iPhone shots that look native to the platform.
    -   **Split Screen**: One model in different colors or different angles of the same garment.
    -   **Offer-Focused**: Grid layouts showing the product with clear "Buy 1 Get 1" or "70% Off" text.
    -   **Eye-Catching/Pattern Interrupt**: Unusual visuals (e.g., a shredded shirt) paired with a hook like "Our shirt won't break, neither will your bank."

## Mistakes / warnings called out
-   **The AOV Trap**: Mixing products with wildly different AOVs in one CBO campaign will cause Meta to spend all the money on low-AOV products because they have lower CPAs, even if they have worse ROAS [07:23, 11:30].
-   **Low Creative Volume**: Avoid running ad sets with only 1–5 creatives; you need at least 6 active winners in a scaling set [12:45].
-   **Bid Cap Dangers**: Don't set massive budgets (e.g., $5k/day) on new Bid Cap campaigns immediately. Start at $500–$1,000/day to ensure the bid is high enough to actually spend [15:51].
-   **Cost Cap vs. Bid Cap**: Cost caps look at 7-day windows and may overspend on single days; Bid Caps are stricter [14:59].

## Top 5 takeaways for a clothing brand media buyer
1.  **Segment by AOV**: Never put a $30 t-shirt and a $300 jacket in the same CBO. Meta will prioritize the t-shirt's lower CPA and ignore the jacket's higher ROAS potential [07:35].
2.  **Kill the Exclusions**: In the post-Andromeda era, broad targeting with zero exclusions (no retargeting, no LALs) performs best because it gives the algorithm the most data to find buyers [05:13].
3.  **Creative is the Targeting**: The "Audience Style Call Out" (e.g., "How to dress Old Money") targets the user more effectively than any interest-based filter [20:45].
4.  **Use Bid Caps for Scaling**: Scaling through Bid Caps ensures you only spend money when Meta can hit your required ROAS, preventing "scaling into a loss" [14:30].
5.  **Research Impressions, Not Dates**: When using Meta Ads Library, sorting by "Highest Impressions" reveals which ads have the highest spend, confirming they are the competitors' true winners [19:45].
