## One-line thesis
You can succeed with a small/tiny Facebook & Instagram ads budget ($3,000/month or less / $600/month or less) by changing your strategy, avoiding constant campaign tinkering, hyper-targeting, modeling what already works via the Meta Ad Library, and focusing on direct-to-offer conversion campaigns rather than brand awareness.

## Method (ordered, step by step)
1. **Define budget classification [01:15]:** Establish whether you are running a "small" budget ($3,000/month or less / $100/day or less) or "tiny" budget ($600/month or less / $20/day or less).
2. **Stop helicopter parenting [01:32]:** Avoid making constant tweaks that reset the learning phase. Set an optimization schedule (e.g., every 7–10 days or longer for small budgets) [03:57].
3. **Get hyper-specific about ideal customers [06:02]:** Target the 1% (or less) of the market that aligns with your product rather than competing with big-budget brands across the whole market.
4. **Copy what already works in your industry [12:22]:** Use the free Meta Ad Library to find competitors running long-running ads (e.g., 6–9 months+) and model their successful hooks, angles, and ad formats.
5. **Use organic content for free ad testing [18:16]:** Post organic content (Reels/static images) on Instagram, identify top-performing content by engagement, add a 5–10 second CTA, and turn them into paid ads.
6. **Be willing to spend more on conversions [06:01]:** Align your customer acquisition cost (CAC) expectations with realistic customer lifetime value (LTV) rather than demanding an unrealistic 10X ROAS out of the gate.
7. **Reduce variables [25:52]:** Consolidate into 1 campaign and 1 ad set to concentrate conversion volume and escape Meta’s learning phase faster.

1. **Consolidate Structure:** Run 1 campaign and 1 ad set for your hero clothing item to aggregate conversion data and escape Meta's learning phase quickly [26:15].
