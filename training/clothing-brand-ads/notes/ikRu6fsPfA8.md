## Title & channel
How to Create a High Converting Email Pop Up Form (2024 Strategy) - WellCopy (Max Well)

## One-line thesis
Switching from a classic single-step pop-up to a multi-step "Quiz" or "Micro-Commitment" form using specific psychological framing ("You've Got") and hidden close buttons can achieve submit rates over 27%.

## Method (ordered, step by step)
1.  **Select the Pop-up Style [00:45]:** Choose between Classic, Micro-commitment, Quiz, or Spin-to-Win based on whether the goal is volume or customer data.
2.  **Define the Offer [06:27]:** Determine if a percentage off, dollar amount off, or free gift is most profitable based on Average Order Value (AOV).
3.  **Setup Device-Specific Forms [09:55]:** Create separate forms for mobile and desktop inside the email service provider (e.g., Klaviyo).
4.  **Configure Timing & Targeting [10:14]:** Set a time delay of 4–8 seconds (6 seconds is the "sweet spot"). Avoid exit-intent on mobile as it frequently misfires.
5.  **Design the Quiz/Micro-Commitment Step [15:45]:** Add an initial question (e.g., "What are you shopping for?") to leverage commitment and personalization bias.
6.  **Optimize Copy [11:11]:** Remove all unnecessary fluff. Change generic buttons like "Continue" to actionable phrases like "Claim Now."
7.  **Implement Segmentation Logic [18:28]:** Use "Submit Hidden Fields" in the button settings to tag the user profile with specific properties (e.g., Gender: Men) based on their quiz selection.
8.  **Apply "Gray Hat" Close Logic [14:00]:** Make the standard 'X' close button transparent and add a text-based rejection button at the bottom (e.g., "No thanks, I’ll pay full price").
9.  **Set up A/B Testing [19:56]:** Run a test between a standard discount (10% off) and a "Mystery Discount" to see which drives higher opt-ins.

## Creative specifics
*   **Copy Formula [12:55]:** Use the phrase **"You've Got [Offer]"** instead of "Get [Offer]." This leverages loss aversion, making the user feel they already own the discount and will lose it if they close the form.
*   **Visual Hierarchy [13:27]:** The offer (e.g., 10% OFF) should be the largest and boldest element on the form.
*   **Button Copy [14:47]:** Use aggressive "Negative CTA" text for the rejection button, such as **"No thanks, I'll pay full price"** to create psychological friction for closing the form.
*   **Quiz Volume [16:03]:** Use as few answers as possible in the quiz (2–3 options) to avoid overwhelming the customer.

## Offer, product, landing page & Shopify CRO
*   **The "Rule of 100" for Offers [06:50]:**
    *   If AOV is **>$100**, use a **Dollar-Off** discount (e.g., "$50 off" sounds more valuable than "10% off" even if the math is the same).
    *   If AOV is **<$100**, use a **Percentage-Off** discount (e.g., "10% off" typically outperforms "$5 off").
*   **Free Gift Strategy [07:55]:** Frame a low-cost production item (e.g., a $2 jewelry holder) as a high-value gift (e.g., "Free $29 Gift") to maintain margins while increasing perceived value.
*   **Mobile vs. Desktop [09:55]:** Mobile forms must be simplified and stripped of large images that crowd the screen, whereas desktop can utilize side images [31:37].

## Mistakes / warnings called out
*   **Generic Copy [11:11]:** "Save on your first order and get email only offers" is called "unnecessary fluff" that causes users to drop off.
*   **The "Continue" Button [11:32]:** Using "Continue" implies there is more work to do; "Claim Now" implies an immediate reward.
*   **Combined Steps [01:58]:** Asking for Email and SMS on the same page significantly hurts conversion rates. Always separate them into Step 1 (Email) and Step 2 (SMS) [02:08].
*   **Exit Intent on Mobile [10:51]:** Misfires frequently and provides a poor user experience.

## Top 5 takeaways for a clothing brand media buyer
1.  **Stop using "Classic" pop-ups:** Implement a **Quiz Step** (Men vs. Women) to collect zero-party data that can be used to trigger personalized email flows.
2.  **Aggressive Copy Framing:** Change all lead magnets to say **"You've Got [Discount]"** to trigger loss aversion.
3.  **The 6-Second Rule:** Don't fire the pop-up immediately; wait 6 seconds so the user can actually see the clothing/brand aesthetic first.
4.  **Negative CTAs:** Add a "No thanks, I'll pay full price" button to make closing the form feel like a financial loss to the user.
5.  **AOV-Based Lead Magnets:** If selling high-end fashion (AOV >$100), test a flat dollar amount (e.g., "$20 off your first order") instead of 10%.
