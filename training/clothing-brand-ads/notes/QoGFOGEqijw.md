This guide outlines a performance-marketing framework for scaling Meta ads by moving beyond "creative volume" and into "creative diversity" and research-backed production.

## Title & channel
- **Title**: Meta Ads: Creative Volume Is Killing Your Account (Do This Instead)
- **Channel**: Fraser Cottrell

## One-line thesis
Success on Meta in the "Andromeda" era requires "Creative Diversity" (different styles/formats) rather than just "Creative Volume" (variations of one style), supported by a two-campaign account structure and research-driven concepting.

## Method (ordered, step by step)
1.  **Research (Phase 1):** Before writing scripts, use Reddit to search your niche (e.g., "best women's shoes") [04:55]. Read comments to find real customer objections and reasons for purchase [05:04].
2.  **Data Extraction (Phase 2):** Export customer reviews and post-purchase surveys [05:31]. Identify "Concrete Knowledge" versus "Assumptions" [05:39].
3.  **Concept Development:** Choose a diverse range of formats based on the goal:
    *   **UGC:** Specific audience segments [00:58].
    *   **Podcast Style:** Educating the customer before they commit [01:04].
    *   **Founder-led:** Building brand trust [01:09].
    *   **Documentary-style VSL:** Mini-product documentary [10:48].
4.  **Production:** Cast and script based on the specific research findings (e.g., addressing why people don't buy similar products) [05:44].
5.  **Account Implementation:** Deploy new creatives into a dedicated "Testing Campaign" to ensure they receive budget [03:33].
6.  **Scaling:** Move assets that prove themselves in the Testing Campaign to the "Scaling Campaign" [03:39].
7.  **Analysis:** Review performance using CPA as the primary filter, followed by CTR and CPM [07:42].

## Meta ads specifics
- **Account Structure:** Use a two-tiered system [03:09]:
    1.  **Testing Campaign:** For new, untested creative. Its job is to give new assets "enough spend" to see results [03:34].
    2.  **Scaling Campaign:** Where winning assets (the "Golden Goose") live and receive the bulk of the budget [03:07, 03:39].
- **The "Similarity Score":** Meta assigns a score to every ad. If ads look too similar (e.g., same UGC with different hooks), Meta flags them as the same asset and limits reach/distribution [01:44].
- **Spend Allocation:** Avoid running new ads in the same campaign as established winners; winners will "eat" all the spend, preventing the new ads from being tested fairly [03:10].

## Creative specifics
- **Creative Volume vs. Diversity:** Volume is 50 variations of the same UGC [00:02]. Diversity is 5-10 completely different concepts (UGC vs. Podcast vs. Static vs. VSL) [00:58].
- **Inspiration Sourcing:** Avoid relying solely on "Meta Ad Library" as it leads to "second-hand ideas" and creates an echo chamber [09:02]. Source from films, TV shows, and books [10:10].
- **UGC Casting:** Easier when based on actual customer demographics found in reviews [05:45].
- **Thumbnails:** Ensure high diversity even in thumbnails so they don't look like a single ad [02:28].
- **Visual Styles:** VSLs (Video Sales Letters) should flow like documentaries [10:22].

## Metrics & benchmarks quoted (with numbers)
- **Primary Metric:** **CPA (Cost Per Acquisition)** [07:42]. Must be evaluated against your specific **LTV (Lifetime Value)** [07:50].
- **Secondary Metric:** **CTR (Click-Through Rate)**. Measures the creative's ability to get users off Meta [07:55].
- **Tertiary Metric:** **CPM (Cost Per Mille)**. High CPMs indicate you are reaching the wrong/expensive audience for that specific creative [08:11].
- **Hook Rate Benchmark:** There is no universal "best." Use your account average (e.g., if you average **25%**, a **30%** hook rate is a win) [07:29].
- **Client Results:** Claims an average of **-30% CPA** after implementing this system [11:42].

## Mistakes / warnings called out
- **The "Fishing" Error:** Repeatedly targeting the same "pond" of customers by using identical ad styles, which eventually exhausts the audience [01:32].
- **Variation Trap:** Thinking that changing just the hook of a UGC video constitutes a "new test" [00:26].
- **Assumption-Based Production:** Writing scripts before doing research on why people actually buy [04:02].
- **Soft Metric Obsession:** Over-optimizing for Hook Rate or Hold Rate when the CPA is unprofitable [07:35]. Hook/Hold rates are "soft metrics" [08:24].
- **Ad Library Echo Chamber:** Copying competitors' ads from 3-6 months ago instead of innovating [09:25].

## Claims vs. evidence
- **Claim:** The agency reduces CPA by 30% [11:42].

## Top 5 takeaways for a clothing brand media buyer
1.  **Stop variation-only testing:** If you're a clothing brand, don't just test 10 different girls wearing the same dress. Test a "stylist" tip video, a "behind the design" founder video, and a high-aesthetic VSL to reach different "pots" of buyers.
2.  **Isolate your tests:** Create a "Testing Campaign" with a set budget to ensure your new seasonal collections or styles actually get seen by the algorithm.
3.  **Mine Reddit for "Vibe":** Search subreddits like r/fashion or r/shoes to find the specific adjectives customers use to describe your competitors' products (e.g., "blister-free," "actually waterproof") and use those as your ad angles.
4.  **Diversify Thumbnails:** Since fashion is highly visual, Meta's "Similarity Score" will kill your reach if all your ads look like standard studio shots. Mix lifestyle, flat lays, and "in the wild" UGC.
5.  **CPA > Hook Rate:** A high hook rate on a "sale" ad is useless if it's attracting low-quality buyers who don't convert. Always judge fashion creative by the cost of the sale first.
