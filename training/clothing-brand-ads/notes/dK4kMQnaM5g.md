## One-line thesis
AI is not a shortcut to copy-paste ads; it is an amplifier that requires human brand expertise, customer data inputs, and structured system prompting to generate high-converting Facebook ad copy in minutes [00:54, 01:07].

---

## Method (ordered, step by step)

1. **Build a Master System Prompt** [01:26, 02:27]
   * Establish a permanent "system prompt" containing brand context, positioning, customer psychology/profile, tone/voice, voice examples, and output formatting rules so the AI retains brand guardrails across all sessions.
2. **Construct a Customer-Informed Brand Brief** [04:22, 04:48]
   * Feed raw, unedited customer language (reviews, DMs, comments, support texts) into Claude to extract:
     * Voice of Customer (exact phrases)
     * Core Feeling / Emotional outcome
     * Specific ideal customer persona ("The One Person")
3. **Generate & Filter Scroll-Stopping Hooks (Pattern Interrupts)** [05:15, 09:50]
   * Prompt Claude using the structure: `[Pattern Interrupt] + [Specific Identity Signal] + [Curiosity Gap]`.
   * Filter out corporate/marketing language in favor of conversational, human-sounding hooks.
4. **Tailor Copy to the Specific Funnel Stage** [06:40, 10:15]
   * Generate distinct copy variations for:
     * **Cold Traffic:** Leads with curiosity and pattern interrupts.
     * **Retargeting:** Leads with urgency and direct call to action (CTA).
     * **Past Purchasers:** Leads with brand identity and insider exclusivity.
5. **Apply Human Sharpening (Human-in-the-Loop)** [08:17, 08:42]
   * Use AI to draft initial creative options 10x faster [08:43], then spend 10 minutes having a human brand expert sharpen and polish the copy to preserve brand voice and edge [08:48].
6. **Extract the Winning System Prompt for Future Use** [10:23]
   * Once a winning ad copy is finalized, prompt Claude to back-engineer a reusable system prompt that captures the exact tone, structure, and constraints used to create it.

### 1. System Prompt Template [02:27, 02:34]
> "You are a direct-response brand strategist and copywriter for [Brand Name]. You think in terms of customer psychology, conversion, and brand identity. You are direct, confident, and concise. You never use corporate language. You write like a founder who knows this customer better than anyone.
> 
> BRAND CONTEXT: [Brand Name] is a [category] clothing brand for [who they are]. Our positioning: [the one sentence no competitor could honestly say]. Price point: [X]-[X]-[X]-[X]. Primary channel: DTC via Shopify, paid social on Meta and TikTok.
> 
> CUSTOMER PROFILE: Identity: [how they see themselves] Purchase motivation: [What they're really buying — the feeling or signal, not the product] Hidden fear: [What they worry about that they'd never say out loud] Language they use: [Exact words and phrases from your customer data] Competitor context: [Who they almost bought from and why they didn't]
> 
> TONE AND VOICE: [Describe your tone in 2-3 sentences. Include what you never say.]
> 
> VOICE EXAMPLES: [Paste 2-3 lines from your best-performing captions or emails]
> 
> OUTPUT RULES:
> • Always provide 3 variants unless told otherwise
> • Never use exclamation marks
> • Always include a CTA in ad copy
> • Never open with a question as the first line
> • Keep ad copy under 125 words unless specified
> • Use line breaks in captions - no walls of text"

### 3. Prompt 2 — Generate 10 Hook Variations [09:50]
> "Using the Brand Brief above, write me 10 Facebook ad hooks for my clothing brand.
> 
> Each hook must follow this exact structure:

## Mistakes / warnings called out

1. **Lazy Prompting / Generic AI Slop:** Typing generic prompts like `"Write a facebook ad for my hoodie"` produces zero personality, zero specificity, and no reason for customers to care [03:24, 03:44, 08:39].
2. **100% Full Automation Without Human Oversight:** Running fully automated AI copy without human review leads to bland, generic DTC-sounding copy with no edge, point of view, or brand voice [08:24, 08:30].
3. **100% Manual Writing:** Writing everything from scratch manually takes too long and prevents rapid testing [09:03].
4. **Marketing Jargon vs. Customer Language:** Using corporate or polished marketing language (e.g., "super soft fabric") fails to stop the scroll compared to verbatim customer review phrasing [06:19, 06:26].

## Top 5 takeaways for a clothing brand media buyer

1. **Set Hard Output Rules in a System Prompt:** Enforce strict formatting constraints (under 125 words, no exclamation marks, no starting with questions, mandatory line breaks) to immediately eliminate "AI slop" formatting [02:34].
2. **Mine Verbatim Customer Words for Hooks:** Feed raw customer reviews, support DMs, and comments directly into Claude. The exact words customers use to describe comfort or fit generate much higher hook retention than polished marketing phrases [04:24, 06:17].
3. **Optimize for the 3-Second Pattern Interrupt:** Use the structure `[Pattern Interrupt] + [Identity Signal] + [Curiosity Gap]` to aim for >30% 3-second video retention, which lowers Meta distribution costs [05:26, 09:50].
4. **Segment Copy by Funnel Stage:** Never run the same copy to cold and retargeting audiences. Prompt Claude specifically for curiosity hooks on cold traffic, urgency/CTA on retargeting, and brand identity on past buyers [06:40, 10:15].
5. **Use AI for Speed, Humans for Edge:** Treat Claude as a draft engine that gives you 90% completed copy in seconds, then spend 10 minutes refining the last 10% to ensure true brand voice and positioning [08:17, 09:14].
