Here is the Meta (Facebook/Instagram) growth system for clothing brands using Claude AI, as detailed by Aaron from BitBranding.

## Title & channel
* **Title:** How To Use Claude AI For Your Facebook Ads (In 2024)
* **Channel:** BitBranding

## One-line thesis
Scaling a clothing brand on Meta now requires moving away from manual audience hacking (interest stacking/bid caps) and using Claude AI to generate highly specific "Struggling Avatar" data that informs broad-targeted, creative-led campaigns [00:15].

## Method (ordered, step by step)
1.  **Define the "Struggling Avatar":** Use Claude to fill out a "Struggling Avatar" framework to identify the core physical and emotional frustrations of the target customer [03:06].
2.  **Generate Creative Hooks and Scripts:** Feed the avatar data into Claude to produce direct-response scripts for UGC or high-quality video [06:25].
3.  **Build a Campaign Brief:** Use Claude to outline the account structure, budget allocation, and testing metrics before opening Ads Manager [13:41].
4.  **Execute Broad Targeting:** Set up a simplified campaign structure where the creative does the targeting for the algorithm [15:54].
5.  **Analyze and Iterate:** Evaluate results after a 72-hour "learning" window based on Cost Per Purchase (CPP) rather than secondary metrics [16:32].

## Meta ads specifics
*   **Campaign Objective:** Always optimize for Sales / Conversions (Purchase event) [15:37]. 
*   **Account Structure:** Simplified into a single campaign with three distinct ad sets [02:08].
*   **Ad Set 1 (Cold Broad):** 60% of total spend. No interest targeting. Let the algorithm find the buyer. This is the "workhorse" [15:39].
*   **Ad Set 2 (Interest Stack):** 25% of total spend. Layered interests (e.g., for a running brand: marathon running, ultramarathon, trail running) [15:43].
*   **Ad Set 3 (Retargeting):** 15% of total spend. Targets website visitors (last 30 days) and video viewers (50%+) [15:53].
*   **Targeting:** Broad targeting (Age, Gender, Location) is preferred because "the creative does the targeting" [08:06].
*   **Bidding:** Uses "Highest Volume" (Auto-bid). Warns against using bid caps as they tell Facebook to find "cheaper clicks, not better customers" [17:49].
*   **Testing Cadence:** Launch 6 creatives across cold ad sets at once [16:11].
*   **Kill/Scaling Rules:** Do not make any decisions based on Day 1 data. Use a 72-hour "checklist" [16:30].

## Creative specifics
*   **Ad Formats:** UGC-style video, Static images (flat lays, lifestyle), and Carousels (product features with pain point captions) [16:11].
*   **Hook Formula:** Must speak directly to the person's frustration in the first 0-3 seconds (e.g., "If your shorts are soaked by mile 10... that's not you. That's your gear.") [12:26].
*   **Ad Body (3-22s):** Connects the product to the desired identity transformation [15:56].
*   **Call to Action (22-30s):** Creates urgency without being "pushy" (e.g., "We only drop limited quantities. Check availability.") [15:56].
*   **Angles Generated:** "The Fabric Problem" (logical/product-led), "You've Earned Better" (identity/aspiration), and "The Smelly Runner" (social pain/scroll-stop) [12:23].

## Claude / AI workflow
Aaron uses **Claude (Sonnet 4.6)** [01:43] and **Whisper Flow AI** [03:26] (voice-to-text) to input complex brand data quickly.

*   **Prompt 1 (Avatar Research):** "Okay, so I need you to act as a marketing expert, and I’m going to give you information about my struggling avatar documents. I want you to take all of this information and the parts that are blank that have questions. I want you to ask those questions to me, and then I’m going to answer them for you so that I can get this input." [03:31]
*   **Data Inputs Provided:** High-end running gear for endurance athletes, 40-60+ miles/week, problems with chafing, odor, and plastic-heavy fabrics [04:08].
*   **Prompt 2 (Script Writing):** "Using this avatar, write me three 30-second direct response video scripts for a Facebook ad. Each script needs a hook that speaks directly to the person's frustration in the first three seconds, a body that connects our product to what they're trying to become, and a call to action that creates urgency without being pushy. Write each hook as if you're stopping someone mid-scroll." [15:56]
*   **Prompt 3 (Campaign Brief):** "Based on the customer avatar, these ad scripts, and the offer, build me a Facebook ad campaign brief. I want you to include the recommended campaign objective (sales), ad set structure, how many creatives to test, what to look for in the first 72 hours, and what metrics tell us it is working or not working." [14:48]

## Mistakes / warnings called out
*   **Bid Caps:** Warns that bid caps fight the algorithm and result in lower-quality customers [17:49].
*   **Interest Stacking:** Called "the old way." It limits the algorithm's ability to find buyers based on creative resonance [00:18].
*   **Over-managing Ad Sets:** Avoid building too many ad sets (e.g., 12+) and manually trying to outsmart the AI [00:22].
*   **Ignoring 72-Hour Window:** Making changes in the first 24 hours restarts the learning phase and ruins performance [16:30].

## Top 5 takeaways for a clothing brand media buyer
1.  **Creative is Targeting:** In 2024, your audience targeting is defined by the words and visuals in your ad, not the toggles in Ads Manager.
2.  **Use Claude for "Inputs":** AI shouldn't just write your ads; it should help you identify the "Struggling Avatar"—the specific pain points that make a customer stop scrolling.
3.  **Simplify Account Structure:** Consolidate into one "Sales" campaign with limited (max 3) ad sets to allow the pixel to exit the learning phase faster.
4.  **The "Workhorse" is Broad:** Your primary budget should go to a Cold Broad ad set with zero interest or lookalike targeting.
5.  **Metrics Focus:** Prioritize LTV (Lifetime Value) and Acquisition Cost over short-term CPMs. A higher CPP for a repeat-buyer customer is better than a low CPP for a one-time discount seeker.
