## One-line thesis
Meta’s Andromeda AI update shifts ad delivery optimization from setting-level audience targeting (interests/lookalikes) to creative-level targeting, requiring fashion brands to scale creative volume, diversification, and freshness without altering core brand values [00:18, 01:45, 10:18].

---

## Method (ordered, step by step)
1. **Understand Meta’s Andromeda Shift [01:45]:** Realize that traditional interest and lookalike targeting at the ad set level no longer drive performance. Creative content itself now serves as the targeting mechanism [03:35].
2. **Understand How Meta Reads Creatives [02:46]:** Meta uses 4 AI engines to dissect each ad: Visual features, Audio, Motion/Movement, and Copy/Text [02:46].
3. **Decouple Brand Values from Brand "Outfits" [10:03]:** Keep core brand values, mission, and ICP identical, while varying the ad presentation format, tone, setting, and style [10:33].
4. **Build a Creative Diversification Matrix [13:20]:** Define target Personas, mapping each to specific Pain Points/Angles, and assign multiple Delivery Formats [13:20].
5. **Calculate & Execute Creative Volume (3P × 3A × 8F Framework) [17:10]:** Multiply 3 Personas × 3 Angles × 8 Formats to produce 72 distinct creative concepts per cycle [17:24].
6. **Maintain a Refresh Cadence [06:47]:** Continuously introduce fresh, net-new concepts (not minor iterations) to prevent audience fatigue and avoid Meta algorithm penalties [07:05].

## Creative specifics
* **The 4 AI Engine Variables analyzed by Meta [02:46]:**
  1. 👀 **Visuals:** Visual features and objects inside the creative [02:50].
  2. 👂 **Audio:** Voiceover, music, and audible cues [02:51].
  3. 🏃💨 **Motion:** Movement, who is in the ad, and action taking place [02:52].
  4. 📝 **Copy:** Spoken text, text overlays, hooks, and primary text [02:56].
* **Engagement Signals Meta tracks to build User Models [08:00]:**
  * Views / Dwell time (how long users linger on a post) [08:08]
  * Clicks and taps [08:12]
  * Shares, likes, comments, DMs [08:14]
  * Zooming in/out (noted that large brands intentionally place small text in top corners like *"look at top left corner"* to force zoom interactions, triggering Meta's engagement signals) [08:17]
  * Screenshots (early tests suggest Meta tracks this) [08:38]

## Mistakes / warnings called out
1. **Running minor iterations of the same ad [04:08]:** Uploading 5 UGC videos featuring the same person, setup, and hook with minor tweaks is seen by Meta as 1 single creative concept, locking you into the same exhausted audience segment [04:14, 05:18].
2. **Confusing LoFi/Diversified Formats with compromising Brand Values [10:03]:** Brand owners fear going "off-brand," leading them to run only one visual style (e.g., minimalist photos on white backgrounds), which severely caps audience reach [11:24].
3. **Neglecting Content Refresh [06:47]:** Running old creatives without introducing fresh concepts results in audience fatigue and explicit delivery penalties by Meta [07:05].

## Top 5 takeaways for a clothing brand media buyer
1. **Stop relying on ad-set interest/lookalike targeting:** Andromeda uses the visual, audio, motion, and copy elements of your ad creative to target specific user segments automatically [01:45, 03:18].
2. **Vary your "brand outfits," not your brand values:** Maintain your brand message and quality, but test diverse execution formats (UGC, lookbooks, iPhone photos, memes, founder videos) to reach distinct consumer groups [10:18, 11:44].
3. **Use the 3P × 3A × 8F diversification matrix:** Build systematic creative pipelines by pairing 3 customer personas with 3 unique marketing angles across 8 ad formats to yield 72 distinct ad concepts [17:10].
4. **Increase net-new creative volume:** Aim for higher monthly creative outputs (e.g., up to 40 net-new concepts/month) to increase your odds of unlocking scalable audience pockets [06:20].
5. **Fresh content is mandatory to avoid penalties:** Without a constant refresh cycle, your account will face ad fatigue and higher CPAs caused by Meta's algorithm penalizing stale ads [06:47, 07:05].
