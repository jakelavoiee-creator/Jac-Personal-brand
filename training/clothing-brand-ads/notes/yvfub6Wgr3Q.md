This breakdown provides the exact Meta ads testing and scaling methodology used by the agency Skaleit, as detailed in the video.

*   **Channel:** Skaleit (Speaker: [Name not provided, but identifies as founder of Skaleit agency])

## One-line thesis
The most effective way to scale Meta ads is to move away from complex manual management (ABO) toward data-consolidated CBO structures that prioritize user engagement metrics and trust Meta’s internal forecasting AI.

## Method (ordered, step by step)
1.  **Define Testing Philosophy [08:52]:** Determine if you are willing to "waste" budget to force testing on specific variables (ABO) or if you want to optimize for immediate efficiency by trusting Meta's AI to pick winners (CBO).
2.  **Select Account Structure [17:30]:** Choose one of four agency-vetted structures based on budget and SKU count (see Meta Ads Specifics).
3.  **Launch Testing Phase [09:20]:** Create ad sets grouping 4–6 creatives per concept, persona, or angle. 
4.  **Monitor Engagement Metrics [05:25]:** Use the "Engagement Rate Ranking" metric to identify which ads Meta's AI will favor for distribution.
5.  **Transition to Scaling [17:50]:** Use Post IDs to move winning creatives into a scaling campaign (usually a single-ad set CBO) to maintain social proof and engagement.
6.  **Analyze and Iterate [19:15]:** Read the data for incrementality rather than just surface-level ROAS, as low-ROAS ads sometimes drive overall account growth.

## Meta ads specifics
*   **Account Structures [17:40 - 20:10]:**
    *   **Structure 1 (ABO Test to CBO Scale):** ABO testing campaign (single ad sets per concept) duplicated into a CBO scaling campaign (1 ad set containing all winners).
    *   **Structure 2 (1 Campaign, 1 Ad Set):** A single campaign and ad set for both testing and scaling. Best for brands spending $1k–$2k/day with single products. Can house 50–60 creatives.
    *   **Structure 3 (1 Campaign, Multiple Ad Sets CBO):** One CBO campaign where each ad set is a separate test. Meta distributes budget to the predicted winners.
    *   **Structure 4 (Multi-Ad Set CBO Test + Scaling Campaign):** Same as Structure 3, but with an additional separate campaign for winners to allow for more aggressive budget control.
*   **Budgets [08:52]:** Testing typically requires spending £200–£300 ($250–$380 approx.) per test to reach statistical significance, depending on CPM.
*   **Bidding [18:10]:** Use "Highest Volume / Lowest Cost" for most structures. Manual bidding (Cost Cap/Bid Cap) should be used in a separate ad account or strictly separated if scaling at very high volumes to avoid performance interference.
*   **Targeting [03:50]:** Emphasis on "Broad" targeting via the creative (Andromeda strategy); let the creative find the audience at the ad level.
*   **Scaling Rules [17:50]:** Duplicate winning ads into the scaling campaign using **Post IDs** to keep all likes, comments, and shares.

## Creative specifics
*   **Volume [09:20]:** 4–6 creatives per ad set in testing.
*   **High-Volume Structure [13:35]:** Up to 50–60 creatives can be run in a single-ad set CBO if the budget is high enough ($1k–$2k/day).
*   **Engagement Strategy [05:40]:** Creative must provide a "good experience" for the user. Meta measures this via engagement; high engagement reduces CPM and CPC.
*   **Variables [17:50]:** Tests should be grouped by a mix of **Persona, Concept, and Angle**.

## Claude / AI workflow
*   **Meta Andromeda [01:47]:** Refers to Meta’s internal AI shift toward ad-level learning.
*   **AI Forecasting [10:30]:** Meta’s AI analyzes every ad at launch and forecasts performance based on existing account data, the target audience, and the creative’s expected engagement.

## Offer, product, landing page & Shopify CRO
*   **Landing Pages [03:30]:** Different ad sets should be used to test specific landing pages to maintain flexibility in the testing phase.
*   **Performance Drivers [08:10]:** The speaker explicitly states that "the main reason why ads work well is usually because of the creatives and the landing page."

## Mistakes / warnings called out
*   **The CBO Trap [06:13]:** Warning that CBO may push budget to an ad with a 2x ROAS because it has better engagement, even if another ad has a 5x ROAS with lower engagement.
*   **Mixing Bidding Types [18:20]:** Do not mix Highest Volume and Manual Bidding (Cost Caps) in the same campaign; it usually leads to poor performance.
*   **Incomplete Testing [14:40]:** Warning against deciding an "angle" doesn't work if you only tested it with a small number of static images and no video variations.
*   **ABO Complexity [07:15]:** Managing 30–50 ABO ad sets is too complex and time-consuming at scale compared to CBO.

## Top 5 takeaways for a clothing brand media buyer
1.  **Prioritize the Engagement Metric [05:25]:** In clothing/fashion, visuals are everything. Check your "Engagement Rate Ranking" custom column; if it's "Below Average," Meta will penalize your reach, regardless of your ROAS.
2.  **Consolidate Data (Andromeda) [02:50]:** Avoid spreading spend across too many ad sets. Group fashion creatives into fewer ad sets to help Meta's AI learn faster which styles appeal to which customers.
3.  **Use Post IDs for Scaling [17:58]:** When a specific outfit or UGC video starts performing, move its Post ID to your scaling campaign to stack social proof (comments/likes), which builds trust for new customers.
4.  **Trust Meta's AI Forecasting [13:40]:** If you launch a CBO and Meta gives 0 spend to an ad, trust that the AI has predicted it won't convert based on initial engagement signals. Don't force spend into it unless you have a high testing budget.
5.  **Focus on Creative, Not Knobs [08:08]:** Instead of spending hours tweaking ABO bid adjustments, spend that time building new "Concepts" (e.g., lifestyle vs. studio) or "Angles" (e.g., "the perfect date night dress" vs. "work-from-home comfort").
